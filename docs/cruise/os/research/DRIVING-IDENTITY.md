# Driving identity: lessons for Cruise OS

Reviewed October 15, 2026. Scope: official manuals, publisher/developer material and explicitly identified criticism. No games were played for this audit; no interface timings, usability percentages or player-population conclusions were measured. This is research before design, not authorization to reproduce another game's interface, music, marks or assets.

## Evidence and boundaries

| Reference | Evidence actually read | Transferable lesson and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gran Turismo 4** | Sony's manual documents category icons on a world map; Home contains Garage, Photo Lab and Diary. Garage combines a vehicle image, specifications and selection; photographs have their own collection. The publisher positions GT4 around automotive lifestyle and history. [Original Sony-authored manual, archived host, printed pp. 16–18](https://db.hfsplay.fr/files/2019/12/02/Gran_Turismo_4_USA_teBOv76.pdf), [official product page](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/products/gt4/) | **Enduring convention:** a car is an object of affection, and collections have a recognizable home. **Styling:** GT4's illustrated map and era-specific chrome are optional. This research does not verify a particular menu soundtrack sequence or music-control mechanism. |
| **Gran Turismo 7** | PlayStation's new-player guide explicitly acknowledges intimidating menu density, introduces facilities progressively, describes Café Menu Books and car appreciation, and distinguishes music-driven replay from driving. Music Rally couples checkpoints to remaining beats. [Official guide](https://blog.playstation.com/2022/03/03/a-quick-guide-to-gran-turismo-7-for-new-racers/), [official replay manual](https://www.gran-turismo.com/us/gt7/manual/replay/01) | **Adopt:** cultural context and a welcoming sense of place. **Improve:** immediate access rather than a progression curriculum. **Reject:** beat countdowns and unlock gates for listening. A cinematic camera is optional; it must not displace the radio. |
| **Forza Horizon 5** | Xbox describes adjustable text/subtitles, independent UI/world color settings, high contrast, notification duration, remapping and disabling moving menu backgrounds. An official expansion article lets players hide its roll indicator without losing the associated game functionality. [Xbox accessibility announcement](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2021/11/04/gaming-for-everyone-the-accessibility-features-of-forza-horizon-5/), [Forza HUD toggle](https://forza.net/news/forza-horizon-5-hot-wheels-now-available/) | **Enduring convention:** the interface adapts to the person and can yield to the world. The FOV additions described in the expansion article are scoped to Hot Wheels, not general proof of every FH5 camera. Neon festival styling is not an accessibility technique. |
| **Vice City / San Andreas** | Rockstar/Take-Two's original soundtrack releases describe selectable genre-specific stations, era/place-specific musical selections, DJ dialogue, jingles and station imaging. Vice City uses an eighties dial; San Andreas describes early-nineties West Coast radio culture. [Vice City, 2002](https://ir.take2games.com/static-files/7dcf68df-b3d5-4b7f-b884-16b941f0a4cc), [San Andreas, 2004](https://ir.take2games.com/static-files/6a323179-2284-47be-a2fd-612905e05835) | **Enduring convention:** a station is a recognizable cultural voice within a place, not just a track list. **Adopt:** identities, deliberate tuning and continuity. **Reject:** copying fictional stations or inserting Cruise dialogue/advertising into someone else's broadcast. Those licensed soundtracks confer no rights on Cruise. |

## Criticism worth designing against

A GT7 player discussion complains that car customization crosses multiple facilities and that changing menus interrupts music. This is an anecdotal thread, not a frequency estimate or proof that every version behaves identically. Its useful warning is concrete: spatial metaphors can multiply trips through navigation. [Player discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/granturismo/comments/1rdi3ps/music_changing_every_menu/)

Can I Play That's launch-era FH5 menu inspection reports that high contrast appears limited to popup titles. This is a specialist accessibility observation, not a current full-game compliance audit. Cruise must check contrast in every actual control state instead of treating a “high contrast” switch as proof. [Accessibility inspection](https://caniplaythat.com/2021/11/04/forza-horizon-5-accessibility-menu-deep-dive/)

A GT4 player asks how to identify a favorite GT Mode menu song; replies disagree about soundtrack coverage and correct earlier recollections. This illustrates attachment and identification friction, not verified track metadata or the claim that jazz improves retention. [Archived player discussion](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/561066-gran-turismo-4/48315063)

## Ranked decisions for World / Radio / Glovebox

1. **Make Radio persistent.** World changes scenery and camera; Glovebox opens personal finds, presets and settings. Neither action restarts playback. Keep station, play state, mute/volume and a clear tuning entry reachable in all three. Avoid separate audio systems disguised as thematic rooms.
2. **Give each destination one responsibility.** World is the drive; Radio is tuning and verified on-air information; Glovebox holds things the listener intentionally keeps. Use these words alongside any icons. The three-part structure is our design hypothesis, not an established convention from the references.
3. **Express culture through real content.** Show the chosen vehicle, recognizable Miami and attributed station identities. Short, optional editorial context can deepen appreciation. Preserve unavailable-metadata wording; never invent an artist or DJ to make the presentation feel complete.
4. **Let controls recede without vanishing.** Offer a calm default, visible focus, readable surfaces, keyboard/touch parity and reduced motion. Essential radio actions remain discoverable; hover-only or gesture-only access is insufficient. Verify portrait, landscape and zoom before adopting ornamental dashboard treatment.
5. **Keep ownership personal and reversible.** Glovebox can feel like GT4's Home through explicit saved objects and recognizable organization, without collecting music taste for ranking or requiring collection goals. Export/delete and clear empty states matter more than trophy-like progress.
6. **Borrow principles, not console complexity.** Reject license gates, upgrade economies, forced tutorials, permanently dense racing HUDs and nostalgia-driven imitation. Cruise is a listening environment; it does not need racing instrumentation to validate its identity.

Reuse the existing [Roadarium/Hop research](../../experience/research/ROADARIUM-HOP.md): bounded geography, intentional routes and portable starting moments are already assessed there, with mobile/performance limitations. Do not repeat that research or infer that console presentation budgets transfer to Next.js/R3F. Prototype navigation and audio continuity first; evaluate styling and retention hypotheses with actual Cruise playtests afterward.
