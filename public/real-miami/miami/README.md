# Miami geography bake

This experimental 5 × 5 km tile is centered at longitude -80.135, latitude 25.790. Coordinates are local meters: x east and z south. The bbox is about 25.76754..25.81246 latitude, -80.15993..-80.11007 longitude. Full OSM ways intersecting the tile are retained; chunk indexing is bounded to the tile, so source geometries can extend beyond it.

`world.json` is an offline rendering bake; `osm-raw.json` retains the original OpenStreetMap response and acquisition timestamp. OpenStreetMap data is © OpenStreetMap contributors, licensed under the Open Database License (ODbL): https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright . The derived database retains the same license. Do not remove the attribution in the experience.

`scripts/bake-miami.mjs` fetches roads, footprints, land, coastlines, and named tourism/historic features with Overpass. It projects features into meters, stitches closed coast rings, clips filled island polygons to the tile, indexes features in 250 m cells, and routes over the principal mutually reachable motor-road graph. The bake uses tagged building heights/levels where available and explicitly labels estimates elsewhere. Rendered generic facades are not surveyed architectural reconstructions.

The touring itinerary starts on Ocean Drive and follows connected OSM streets for 19.643 km. It preserves one-way and motor access tags; immediately retraced waypoint spurs are removed. A return waypoint at Ocean Drive/13th Street ensures the landmark cluster is passed from its street fronts. Turn restriction relations, temporary closures, and traffic signal timing are not modeled, so this is not a navigation system. A closed itinerary eventually repeats; it is a substantially longer connected tour, not unlimited free driving. The route helper rounds corners with a quadratic within a maximum 3 m intersection trim and preserves a continuous seam. Camera/vehicle speed must slow according to curvature.

`elevation.json` contains a 51 × 51 sampled DEM at 100 m spacing. Four raw AWS Terrain Tiles Terrarium PNGs are retained. Terrain source attribution is preserved in `ELEVATION-ATTRIBUTION.md`, with its original link: https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md . The sampled values include water and surface/building effects and range -6 to 55 m; rendered roads currently keep a flat sea-level datum. Acquired elevation does not imply accurate rendered road or bridge elevations.

Pixel Path (https://github.com/jonwheatley/pixel-path) is MIT licensed, copyright 2026 Jon Wheatley; its license is retained in `public/pixel-path-LICENSE.txt`. Compatible techniques adapted here are offline cached OSM baking, local metric projection, real footprint placement, geographic chunk indexing, and automated checks. Its pixel artwork is not used. `scripts/pixel-path-lib.mjs` is the MIT source utility used to decode Terrarium PNGs; a reference bake is retained for traceability.

`advertisingPlots` contains geographically persistent, experiment-only demonstration placements MB-HOUSE-001 through MB-HOUSE-003 on the east sidewalk of Ocean Drive. They are derived from road coordinates, use no external campaigns, and do not represent production campaign assignments.

Reproduce with `node scripts/bake-miami.mjs`, `node scripts/bake-miami-elevation.mjs`, and `node scripts/verify-miami-geography.mjs`. The first script reuses the saved OSM response if present; acquisition is not performed during browser driving. `validation.json` and `route-quality.json` contain actual bake and continuity results.
